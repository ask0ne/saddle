01 — PALETTE

05 — REFINED MARK

Profile, five ways

The chosen mark, pushed through five renderings before picking a final weight — all in Tack, all shown at 32px, since a single stroke-width choice looks very different once it's actually next to the saddle-brown ground it'll live on.

Line

The original single-stroke path. Elegant, a little thin once it's small and alone in a busy menubar.

Bold

Same path, heavier stroke. Holds up at 18px against a cluttered system menubar far better than Line does.

Solid

Filled silhouette, no stroke at all. Reads as a confident, weighty mark even at 16px — closest to a real saddle-leather brand mark, at the cost of losing the "sketched" quality Line has.

Stitched

Line, plus a second brass stroke standing in for saddle-stitching across the seat. Two-color, most literal-craft of the five — needs a light background or dark menubar to actually read as two colors, not one blur.

Ringed

Line inside a faint containing circle — the "badge" read of Stamp, without the monogram. A middle ground if Profile alone feels too loose as a standalone app-icon shape.

decided

Bold, everywhere — menubar icon, splash, .dmg background, app icon. One weight, one mark, fully iconic rather than split across contexts.

07 — LANGUAGE

Naming the two modes

Confirmed: saddle (pill) → stable (window). "Boarded" and "take off" worked as placeholders but mixed metaphors — boarding is nautical/aviation, saddle is equestrian. Two alternates kept below for the record, in case either tab or copy voice ever wants a different register.

Pill stateFull windowReasoning
saddle ✓ stable ✓ Confirmed. Saddle = tacked up and riding light, exactly what the pill is. Stable = where you go to see the whole herd at once, at rest. Verb/noun pair, both one word, both instantly readable without the metaphor needing to be explained.
Riding Barn Slightly more casual register than Saddled/Stable — "Riding" reads a touch more active/continuous, "Barn" a touch warmer than "Stable." Same pairing logic, different tone.
Mounted Paddock "Mounted" is the most literal opposite of "dismounted" — technically precise, reads slightly more formal/technical than the other two options. "Paddock" (an open pen you survey the herd from) over "Corral," since Corral is already a palette name in this doc and reusing it for a mode would overload the word.
in code, already

The Rust commands are already named enter_pill_mode / enter_app_mode — cheap to rename to enter_saddle_mode / enter_stable_mode so the code reads the same way the product talks about itself.

the three tabs

stable (default — the fleet, at rest or not) and hooves (settings — what a horse stands on and gets shod with; toggling models/tools is exactly that kind of maintenance) were both already named. For the about/profile section: papers — a horse's papers are its registration and pedigree, the literal "who made this, what version, whose it is" document. One word, stays inside the metaphor, and doesn't double-book a word already spent elsewhere in this doc (unlike brand, which is already a palette name, or tack, already the chosen palette). Alternate if papers reads too formal: groom (the person who cares for the horse — closer to "the person behind this" than "the document about it").

09 — APP ICON

The big icon — Dock, Finder, .dmg

Different job from the 18px menubar glyph: this one has room to breathe, gets seen at 128px in the Dock and 512px in Finder, and is the very first thing anyone sees when the .dmg opens. Bold's silhouette, four treatments of the icon canvas around it.

Flat

Solid umber ground, the mark alone, no depth tricks. Quiet and confident — closest in spirit to the menubar glyph, just scaled up. Risks reading a little plain against Dock neighbors that lean on gradients.

Warm ✓

Confirmed. A soft diagonal gradient plus a faint glow behind the mark — the lighting convention most native macOS icons actually use. Brass instead of red here, since the warmer accent sits better against a lit ground.

Stamp

A brass ring badge, mark inside it — the tooling-iron read from the original icon set, given room to actually work at this size (it was too busy at 18px; here it isn't). Most "crafted object," most premium-feeling of the four.

Poster

Solid's filled silhouette, blown up to fill most of the canvas — bold and graphic, the Raycast/Arc school of icon design. Boldest of the four at a glance, least detail if judged up close at 512px.

confirmed — in the dock

Warm, at actual Dock scale next to placeholder neighbors — the deciding test, and it held up. This is now the app icon: Dock, Finder, Launchpad, and the .dmg below all use it.
